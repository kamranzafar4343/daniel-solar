PE Stamps for Solar Designs: What, Why, and When Do You Need Them?

Getting a solar permit approved by the local building department (Authority Having Jurisdiction, or AHJ) requires structured, detailed drawings that comply with local building and electrical codes. However, for many residential and commercial installations, simply submitting clean drawings is not enough. You will often need the stamp of a licensed Professional Engineer (P.E.).

In this guide, we break down what a P.E. stamp is, the difference between structural and electrical reviews, when they are mandatory, and how to acquire them quickly to keep your project installation timeline on track.

1. What is a Professional Engineer (P.E.) Stamp?

A Professional Engineer (P.E.) stamp is a seal issued by a state licensing board that certifies a design has been reviewed and approved by a registered engineer. By stamping a solar permit plan set, the engineer takes legal responsibility for the design's structural safety, wind load compliance, and electrical safety under local codes (such as the NEC and International Building Code).

Unlike draftspersons, licensed P.E.s have completed strict education, multi-stage testing, and years of field experience. They verify that the proposed solar panels, racking attachments, and electrical connections will not compromise the building's integrity or safety.

2. Structural Stamps vs. Electrical Stamps

Depending on the building department and the complexity of the installation, your solar plan set may require one or both types of stamps:

A. Structural P.E. Stamp

A structural engineer reviews the load-bearing capacity of the building's roof to ensure it can support the weight of the new PV modules and racking equipment (dead load), as well as environmental forces such as wind uplift and snow accumulation (live loads). They verify:

  • Rafter sizes, spacing, and span limits (using IBC/IRC guidelines).
  • Anchor/lag bolt attachment points to the roof structure (assuring proper embedment).
  • Ground mount foundation designs (concrete pier sizing and wind uplift resistence).
  • Racking and clamp manufacturer rating certifications.

B. Electrical P.E. Stamp

An electrical engineer reviews the electrical single-line or three-line diagram (SLD) to ensure the system safely integrates with the existing main panel and the utility grid. They calculate and verify:

  • Conductor sizing and continuous current ratings (applying NEC 310.15 continuous derating).
  • Overcurrent protection devices (OCPD) sizing.
  • Busbar load limits under the NEC 705.12 120% rule.
  • Energy storage clearances and supply-side tap configurations.

3. When Does an AHJ Require a P.E. Stamp?

While some jurisdictions allow standard residential solar designs on standard roofs to proceed without engineering seals, several factors trigger an automatic requirement for a P.E. stamp:

  • Ground Mount Systems: Because they lack a pre-approved roof structure, ground-mount designs require soil classification and foundation calculations stamped by a structural engineer.
  • Tile, Metal, or Flat Roofs: Installing solar on heavy tile (concrete or clay), metal seam roofs, or commercial flat roofs with ballast weights almost always triggers a structural review.
  • Older Homes: Houses built before modern building codes (typically older than 30–40 years) frequently require structural verification of rafters.
  • High Wind or Heavy Snow Regions: Coastlines (with wind speeds above 115 mph) and mountainous regions require localized structural engineering to withstand uplift and heavy loads.
  • Commercial & Utility Scale: 100% of commercial PV carport canopies, flat-roof ballasted arrays, and utility installations require stamped plan sets.

4. Wet Ink Stamps vs. Secure Digital P.E. Seals

Engineers historically signed drawing sheets with physical "wet" ink stamps. Today, almost all modern permitting is digital. Let's look at the difference:

Format Verification Process Processing Time AHJ Acceptance
Secure Digital Seal Verifiable SHA-256 digital signature embedded inside PDF files. Instant (sent via email) 90%+ of modern online permit portals (like Accela and SolarAPP+).
Wet Ink Stamp Engineer physically stamps and hand-signs paper sheets. Requires courier shipping Rarely required now (except in strict local building departments).
