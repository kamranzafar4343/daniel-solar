NEC 2023 Solar & Storage Code Changes: What Contractors Must Know

An essential engineering breakdown of National Electrical Code (NEC 2023) articles 690, 705, and 706, detailing revised busbar rules, rapid shutdown criteria, and Power Control System (PCS) allowances.

As jurisdictions across the United States adopt the 2023 National Electrical Code (NEC / NFPA 70), solar installers and electrical designers face substantial updates to system architecture, overcurrent protection sizing, and disconnect accessibility.

Key revisions in Article 690 (Solar Photovoltaic Systems), Article 705 (Interconnected Power Production Sources), and Article 706 (Energy Storage Systems) directly impact plan review approvals and field inspection passes.

1. Rapid Shutdown Revisions (NEC 690.12)

Rapid shutdown requirements have been refined to harmonize array-boundary voltage drops with emergency responder protocols:

  • Initiation Device Location: Must be located on the exterior of the building in a readily accessible position or integrated with the main service disconnect.
  • Clear Visual Indicator: Devices must have an unmistakable indicator showing whether the rapid shutdown system is in the "OFF" (initiated) or "ON" (operational) state.
  • Controlled Conductor Voltages: Limits remain at 30V within 30 seconds inside the 1-foot boundary and 80V within the array perimeter.

⚡ Engineering Insight: Power Control Systems (PCS) Under NEC 705.13

Under NEC 2023, certified Power Control Systems (UL 1741 PCS) can actively curtail solar or battery discharge to prevent panelboard overloading, allowing installers to add large battery systems without requiring costly 400A service upgrades.

2. Interconnection & Busbar Rules (NEC 705.12)

Section 705.12 governs load-side and supply-side connections with critical clarifications:

  1. Opposite End Busbar Rule (120%): When the backfeed breaker is at the opposite end of the busbar from the primary source, total connected generation can equal $1.2 \times \text{Busbar Ampacity} - \text{Main Breaker}$.
  2. Sum Rule (100%): Where multiple breakers are distributed, the sum of all breaker ratings (excluding main breaker) must not exceed busbar ampacity unless PCS controls are installed.
  3. Supply-Side Tap Clearances (705.11): Splicing inside meter/main combination panels must maintain strict wire bending spaces per NEC 312.6.

3. Energy Storage System (ESS) Disconnects (NEC 706)

Dedicated disconnect switches for battery storage systems must now provide:

  • Line-of-sight location within 50 feet of the ESS enclosure.
  • Lockable in the open position per NEC 110.25.
  • Permanent phenolic directory plaque indicating all connected power sources.

4. California Title 24 & AHJ Adoption Status

In California, local building departments enforce Title 24 Part 3 (California Electrical Code), which incorporates the NEC with state amendments. Ensuring plan sets explicitly cite current edition codes avoids immediate plan check redlines.
