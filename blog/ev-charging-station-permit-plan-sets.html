Commercial EV Charging Station Permitting: Electrical Engineering & Load Math

A complete technical guide for commercial EV Charging Infrastructure permit plan sets, Level 2 and DC Fast Charger (DCFC) load calculations, NEC Article 625 compliance, and ADA accessibility design.

With corporate sustainability pledges, state EV mandates, and fleet electrification accelerating across the United States, commercial electric vehicle supply equipment (EVSE) installations have grown exponentially. However, deploying Level 2 charging banks or Level 3 DC Fast Chargers (DCFC) in retail centers, office parks, and industrial depots requires complex municipal permitting and utility engineering.

Permit plan sets for commercial EV charging hubs must comply with NEC Article 625 (Electric Vehicle Power Transfer System), state building energy codes (such as California CALGreen / Title 24), and local ADA accessibility rules. Below is an engineering overview of the required documentation.

1. Level 2 vs. Level 3 DC Fast Charger (DCFC) Power Sizing

Electrical engineers must first evaluate existing building transformer capacity and continuous load limits based on equipment specification:

  • Level 2 Chargers (208/240V AC): Typically output 7.2 kW to 19.2 kW per port (30A to 80A continuous current). A dual-port 48A charger requires a dedicated 60A 2-pole circuit breaker.
  • Level 3 DC Fast Chargers (480V 3-Phase AC): Output 50 kW to 350 kW+ per dispenser. A bank of four 150 kW DC fast chargers represents a 600 kW load addition—often requiring a dedicated utility pad-mounted transformer and 1200A or 2000A 480V service switchboard.

2. NEC Article 625 Continuous Load Calculations

Under NEC 625.41, electric vehicle charging equipment is classified as a continuous load. The branch circuit overcurrent protection device (OCPD) and feeder conductors must be sized at 125% of the maximum load current.

Formula: Sizing Feeders for Commercial EVSE Banks

\text{Minimum Feeder Ampacity} = \sum (\text{Continuous EVSE Load Amps} \times 1.25)

For automated Load Management Systems (ALMS) under NEC 625.42, engineers can specify energy management controllers to dynamically balance power across multiple ports, avoiding costly utility service upgrades.

3. Site Plan & ADA Accessibility Compliance

In addition to electrical single line diagrams, municipal plan checkers mandate detailed civil and architectural site plans detailing parking space dimensions and ADA accessibility:

  • ADA Van Accessible Stalls: Must feature an 11-foot wide parking space with an adjacent 5-foot access aisle, maximum 2% cross-slope, and bollards protecting equipment.
  • Operable Controls Height: Charging connectors, touchscreens, and credit card readers must be situated between 15 inches and 48 inches above finished grade (ICC A117.1).
  • Trenching & Underground Conduit: Civil plans must detail underground trench burial depths (24 inches minimum for rigid conduit under vehicle traffic per NEC Table 300.5) and slurry backfill specs.

4. Required Plan Set Drawings for Municipal Approval

A jurisdiction-ready commercial EVSE permit submittal package engineered by our team includes:

  • Cover Sheet & Code Summary: Scope of work, CALGreen EV spaces, equipment model specifications, and UL 2202 / UL 2594 listings.
  • Civil Site & Striping Plan: Parking layout, ADA access routes, bollard details, and trenching paths.
  • Electrical Single Line Diagram (SLD): Service entrance, transformer specs, main switchboard, distribution panel, EVSE breakers, ALMS controls, and wire/conduit sizing.
  • Electrical Load Calculations: Existing building peak demand plus proposed EVSE continuous load per NEC Article 220.
