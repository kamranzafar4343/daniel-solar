How to Avoid AHJ Plan Check Rejections: A Guide for Solar Installers

Table of Contents
  • 1. The High Cost of Permitting Delays
  • 2. Top 3 Reasons for Plan Check Rejections
  • 3. Critical Structural Details to Include
  • 4. Essential Electrical Diagrams and Code Labels
  • 5. Establishing a First-Pass Engineering Workflow

For solar contractors, permit delays represent one of the largest bottlenecks in the project pipeline. When a municipal building department (AHJ) issues a corrections list (or "redlines"), it sends the design back to the drawing board. This stalls installation schedules, increases soft costs, and harms customer relationships.

The majority of these plan check corrections are entirely preventable. By understanding the specific code compliance details that municipal plan checkers search for, engineers can deliver drawings that pass review on the very first submittal.

1. The High Cost of Permitting Delays

Every day a plan set sits in a plan checker's correction queue is a day your capital is locked up. Studies of solar soft costs show that permit revisions add an average of 2 to 4 weeks to a project's timeline and cost contractors hundreds of dollars in engineering re-drafting fees and admin hours.

2. Top 3 Reasons for Plan Check Rejections

Our review of hundreds of building department correction lists highlights three recurring problem areas:

Rejection Area Common Oversight Code Reference
1. Roof Access Pathways Missing the required 3-foot clearance boundaries on ridges, hips, and valleys. IFC 1205 / CFC 605.11
2. Busbar Overloads Exceeding safety limits of main panel busbars (violating 120% rule calculations). NEC 705.12
3. Racking Attachments Failing to document structural anchor embedment depth or rafter spacing sizes. IBC Chapter 16 / CRC Chapter 8

3. Critical Structural Details to Include

To pass structural review, drawings must clearly show how the solar modules attach to the building. Ensure your plan set documents:

  • Anchor Details: Specify lag bolt diameter, minimum embedment depth (typically 2.5 inches into the center of structural rafters), and fastener spacing.
  • Roof Plan Views: Include rafter sizes (e.g., 2x4, 2x6), rafter spacing, and roofing material layers.
  • Racking Certifications: Attach manufacturer UL 2703 structural testing reports to verify integrated grounding and load capacities.

4. Essential Electrical Diagrams and Code Labels

Electrical diagrams must provide a clear single-line overview of the power routing. Key elements include:

  • Conductor Ampacity Sizing: Detail wire sizing calculations, accounting for continuous load multiplication factor (1.25 under NEC 690.8) and temperature conduit derating.
  • Grounding & Bonding: Clearly depict continuous equipment grounding conductors (EGC) and grounding electrode conductor (GEC) pathways.
  • Code-Compliant Placards: Include detailed drawings of all NEC-required warning labels, specify color schemes (red background with white lettering), and define placement locations (e.g., at main service disconnects).
