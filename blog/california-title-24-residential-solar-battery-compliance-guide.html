California Title 24 Residential Solar & Battery Compliance Guide

Navigating 2022 & 2025 Energy Code requirements, prescriptive sizing equations, battery storage compliance, and HERS verification.

Under the California Building Energy Efficiency Standards (Title 24, Part 6), newly constructed single-family homes, detached accessory dwelling units (ADUs), and multifamily residential buildings are legally mandated to incorporate on-site solar photovoltaic (PV) systems, with battery energy storage system (ESS) readiness and integration playing an increasingly pivotal role in compliance calculations under the 2022 and 2025 Energy Code cycles.

For builders, solar contractors, and architectural engineers, meeting Title 24 requirements requires precise calculation of minimum PV system capacities, compliance software modeling, and accurate preparation of California Energy Commission (CEC) compliance certificates (CF1R/CF2R/CF3R). This engineering guide outlines the exact compliance pathways, sizing formulas, and permit submittal criteria.

1. Prescriptive PV Sizing: Equation 150.1-C Walkthrough

Under Section 150.1(c)14, the prescriptive minimum DC capacity for a residential solar PV system is determined by conditioned floor area and regional climate factors:

Title 24 Equation 150.1-C Formula:

kW_PV = (CFA × A) / 1000 + (N_DU × B)

Where:
• CFA = Conditioned Floor Area of the dwelling in square feet.
• A = PV Capacity factor based on California Climate Zone (CZ 1 to 16).
• B = Dwelling unit scaling factor based on Climate Zone.
• N_DU = Total number of dwelling units.

Consider a 2,500 sq. ft. single-family home located in Climate Zone 7 (San Diego). From CEC Table 150.1-C, factor A is 0.61 and factor B is 1.25:

  • kW_PV = (2,500 × 0.61) / 1000 + (1 × 1.25)
  • kW_PV = 1.525 + 1.25 = 2.775 kW DC Minimum PV System Size

2. Prescriptive vs. Performance Compliance Pathways

Design teams can satisfy Title 24 energy mandates through one of two distinct compliance approaches:

Feature Prescriptive Pathway (§150.1(c)) Performance Pathway (§150.1(b))
Methodology Direct formula calculation (Equation 150.1-C) Computer energy simulation (CBECC-Res / EnergyPro)
Trade-Off Flexibility None: Must install exact calculated PV capacity High: Battery storage, heat pumps, or higher insulation can trade off PV size
Battery Integration Battery storage is optional but encouraged Battery storage (ESS) earns significant Energy Design Rating (EDR) credits
Required Output Standard CF1R-PVB-01-E form Comprehensive CBECC-Res XML compliance package

3. Battery Storage Readiness & JA12 Mandatory Requirements

For all single-family new construction where battery storage is not installed at the time of construction, the 2022/2025 Energy Code mandates Energy Storage Ready infrastructure under Section 150.0(s):

  1. Dedicated ESS Space: A reserved indoor or outdoor location measuring at least 3 ft × 5 ft with required structural support and NFPA 855 spatial clearances.
  2. Subpanel or Busbar Reservation: A dedicated 225A busbar service panel or a reserved 4-space subpanel location adjacent to the main service panel.
  3. Interconnecting Conduit: Minimum 1-inch continuous raceway between the main panel and the designated ESS location.

4. Title 24 Compliance Forms & HERS Verification Workflow

Securing a Certificate of Occupancy from California municipal building departments requires a rigid three-stage documentation process:

  • CF1R-PVB-01-E (Design Phase): Generated by the design engineer and included on Sheet T24-01 of the permit plan set for plan-check approval.
  • CF2R-PVB-01-E (Installation Phase): Completed and signed by the licensed electrical contractor (C-10) certifying equipment matches approved drawings.
  • CF3R-PVB-01-E (Verification Phase): Independent on-site inspection and shading verification conducted by a certified HERS Rater (CHEERS / CalCERTS registry).

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is the prescriptive formula for residential solar sizing under California Title 24 Section 150.1(c)14?

The required minimum DC PV system rating (kW_PV) is calculated using Equation 150.1-C: kW_PV = (CFA x A) / 1000 + (N_DU x B), where CFA is the conditioned floor area in square feet, N_DU is the number of dwelling units, and A and B are climate zone factors published in Title 24 Reference Appendices JA11.

Are Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs) required to install solar PV under Title 24?

Yes, newly constructed detached ADUs must meet Title 24 solar PV requirements. However, attached ADUs, conversions of existing space (such as garage conversions), and additions are generally exempt from mandatory solar PV requirements under Section 150.1(c)14 Exception 1.

What compliance documentation must be submitted to the AHJ for Title 24 solar compliance?

Permit submittals require the CF1R-PVB-01-E (Certificate of Compliance) generated from CEC-approved software (such as CBECC-Res or EnergyPro), followed post-installation by the CF2R-PVB-01-E (Certificate of Installation) and CF3R-PVB-01-E (HERS Certificate of Verification).
